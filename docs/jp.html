Inflation Context

After decades of deflation and ultra-low inflation, Japan has experienced a significant shift since 2022. Inflation has exceeded the Bank of Japan's 2% target consistently for the first time in years, driven by yen depreciation, rising import costs, and increasing domestic wage pressures.

In March 2024, the BoJ ended its negative interest rate policy and yield curve control framework, marking a historic shift in Japanese monetary policy. The BoJ has since continued gradual policy normalization as inflation remains above target.

Bank of Japan Inflation Target

The Bank of Japan set a price stability target of 2 percent in terms of the year-on-year rate of change in the consumer price index (CPI) in January 2013.

The BoJ aims to achieve this target in a sustainable and stable manner, accompanied by wage increases. The Bank considers that achieving the target requires not just a temporary rise in inflation but a virtuous cycle of rising wages and prices.

After ending negative interest rates in March 2024, the BoJ has indicated it will continue to adjust monetary policy as needed to sustainably achieve the 2% target.

Source: Bank of Japan, "The Price Stability Target" and Monetary Policy Statements
