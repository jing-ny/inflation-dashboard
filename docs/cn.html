Inflation Context

China has experienced persistent low inflation and deflationary pressures since 2023, driven by weak domestic demand, property sector stress, and excess manufacturing capacity. This stands in contrast to the elevated inflation seen in Western economies during the same period.

The People's Bank of China (PBoC) has responded with monetary easing, including reserve requirement ratio (RRR) cuts and interest rate reductions, while the government has implemented fiscal stimulus measures.

Inflation Target Framework

Unlike most advanced economies, China does not have a formal inflation targeting framework. Instead, the government sets an annual CPI target of around 3% in the Government Work Report delivered at the National People's Congress each March.

The People's Bank of China's mandate focuses on maintaining monetary and financial stability, supporting economic growth, and keeping the RMB exchange rate stable. Price stability is one objective among several, rather than the primary target as in inflation-targeting central banks.

Source: PBoC Law of the People's Republic of China
