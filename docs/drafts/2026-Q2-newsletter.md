# June 2026 Update: The Producer Got Hot

Q1 closed on a cliffhanger: was March's oil shock temporary, or the start of a harder path? Q2 answered. The shock passed through — and the surprise is where it landed.

---

## The Big Picture

Q1's working thesis was that energy importers carried the inflation risk while producers were buffered. Q2 broke that thesis on its clearest test case. The United States — a net energy producer — is now the hottest major economy, with headline CPI at **4.25% in May**, up from 3.26% in March and 2.41% in February — a ~1.8pp climb in three months. The data does not explain it. Tariffs are floated in the source notes but unconfirmed; what's certain is the direction, not the cause. Core CPI sits lower at 2.82%, but headline PCE has already reached 3.77% in April — above the Fed's own 2.7% projection for 2026, which now looks stale.

---

## Q2 Highlights

**The US upended the producer story.** The economy that should have been insulated is the one running hottest, against a 2% target, with the cause contested. The Fed held at 3.50–3.75% and its March dot plot still pencils in one cut this year — a stance the incoming prints are straining.

**Europe and Canada took the pass-through.** Euro Area headline rose to **3.2% in May** (provisional), from 1.7% in January. Canada jumped 0.4pp in a single month to **3.23% in May**. The ECB held at 2.00% and revised its 2026 HICP staff projection up to roughly 3% over the quarter; the BoC held at 2.25%. These are the energy-importer economies Q1 flagged, behaving as flagged.

**The UK didn't conform.** While its peers accelerated, UK CPI held flat-to-down — **2.8% in May**, matching April and below March's 3.3%. The BoE held at 3.75% and, rather than a single projection, runs scenarios putting 2026 CPI somewhere in a 3.1–3.6% range. The one major that broke from the pattern.

**SARB hiked into the shock.** The South African Reserve Bank raised +25bp to **7.00% in May**, citing Middle East oil and food pressure, with CPI at 4.5% and rising. Its model sees a 2026 average of 4.4%, back to the 3% target only by 2028. Australia, off its peak at **4.2% in April** but still above the 2–3% band, held at 4.10% — a pause after back-to-back hikes in February and March.

**Asia stayed subdued.** Japan drifted up to **1.5% in May**, still below its 2% target, though the BoJ — holding at 0.75% on an 8–1 April vote, with a 2.8% core projection — points to further gradual hikes. China was flat at **1.2% in May** on persistently weak demand, and India ran below its 4% target at 3.48% in April. These anchor the quiet end of the distribution.

The IMF's April WEO — the first post-conflict global forecast Q1 said to watch — arrived and revised 2026 global headline inflation up to **4.4%**, with a severe-scenario alternative above 6%. The largest country revisions landed on Australia and New Zealand (+1.0pp each), then the US, UK and Euro Area (+0.7 to +0.8pp). The shock repriced the whole forecast surface, not one corner of it.

---

## What to Watch in Q3

- Whether US headline keeps climbing, and whether the Fed's single-cut path survives contact with it.
- Whether Europe and Canada's pass-through has peaked or has further to run.
- Whether the UK stays the outlier or finally converges upward.
- Japan's hike trajectory if core holds near the BoJ's 2.8% projection.
- Any narrowing between the IMF's 4.4% baseline and its >6% severe scenario.
