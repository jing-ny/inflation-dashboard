# Inflation Newsletter — 2026-08-22

The largest single move this period is China's — half a point of further disinflation, covered below. The more consequential one is a widening gap between the world's two biggest advanced-economy prints. US CPI eased to **3.36%** in July from 3.53% in June (−0.17pp). The Euro Area moved the other way, to **2.93%** from 2.73%. Neither move clears a materiality threshold on its own, but they push the two blocs toward each other from opposite directions — and away from where their central banks published forecasts.

Canada is the quieter surprise. July CPI came in at **3.03%**, up 0.23pp from June and above the BoC's own July projection of 2.5% for 2026, published the same month the data landed. The Bank held at 2.25% and flagged trade uncertainty around the US tariff review; a third of a point of drift is not a policy trigger, but it is the wrong direction for a bank forecasting inflation "near 2% target."

China is where the data actually moved. July CPI came in at **0.5%**, down half a point from June's 1.0% and the weakest print since January — less than half the IMF's 1.2% projection for the year, and far below the NBS's own 3.0% target. The 1Y LPR has sat at 3.00% for ten months; the April WEO's line on China is "deflationary pressures amid weak domestic demand." Japan's June reading was **1.7%**, below the BoJ's July outlook of 2.5% for the year; the BoJ held at 0.75% on an 8–1 vote and signalled further hikes if the outlook holds. India's most recent available print is **3.48% for April**, and South Korea's is **2.2% for March** — both stale enough that the next releases matter more than the levels.

The April 2026 WEO put global inflation at 4.4% for the year, with broad upside revisions across advanced economies attributed to energy pass-through (US +0.8pp, EA +0.7pp, UK +0.7pp, AU/NZ +1.0pp). Its severe scenario runs above 6%.

Three divergences worth tracking. **Australia**: the RBA's August forecast of 3.3% sits a full 0.7pp below the IMF's 4.0%, with the cash rate already at 4.10% after two hikes this year and June CPI at 3.8%. **Euro Area**: the ECB's June revision to 3.0% now runs above the IMF's 2.6%, and July's uptick leans the ECB's way. **UK**: the April MPR abandoned a central projection for scenarios spanning 3.1–3.6% this year — June CPI at 2.6% is below all of them.
