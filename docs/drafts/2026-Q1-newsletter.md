# March 2026 Update: Q1 Looked Simpler in January

Q1 started as a simple disinflation story. It did not end that way.

---

## The Big Picture

Through January, most of the world was cooling on schedule. US inflation was 2.4%, the Euro Area at 1.7%, Japan below the BoJ's 2% target for the first time since 2022. Then the Iran conflict in March disrupted the Strait of Hormuz, oil briefly hit $120/bbl, and central bank forecasts had to be rewritten.

Q2 will test whether March was a temporary shock, or the start of a harder inflation path.

---

## Q1 Highlights

**Australia hiked twice.** The RBA raised rates in both February and March, bringing the cash rate to 4.10% — making it the only major economy tightening. Trimmed mean inflation hit 3.4% in the December quarter, well above the 2-3% target band. The RBA doesn't expect inflation back in range until early 2027.

**Canada fell fast.** CPI dropped from 2.3% in January to 1.8% in February — largely base effects from the GST/HST tax holiday ending. The BoC, already at 2.25% after an aggressive cutting cycle, now has to weigh whether tariff uncertainty will reverse the trend.

**China turned positive.** CPI rose to 1.3% in February from 0.2% in January — the first convincingly positive print in over a year. Likely seasonal (Lunar New Year), but worth watching. The IMF still projects only 0.7% for the full year.

**The war repriced forecasts.** The ECB revised 2026 HICP from 1.9% to 2.6% (+0.7pp) — almost entirely energy-driven. The Fed moved more modestly, PCE from 2.5% to 2.7%, reflecting US energy independence. The gap between post-conflict central bank forecasts and pre-conflict IMF projections (WEO January 2026) tells you who's exposed: energy importers carry the risk, producers are buffered.

**Most central banks froze.** Coming into Q1, most were in cutting mode. The Iran conflict gave every hawk a reason to pause. The Fed, ECB, BoE, BoC, RBNZ, and BoJ all held in their latest meetings. Only the RBA moved — upward, twice.

---

## What to Watch in Q2

- **IMF April WEO** — First post-conflict global forecast. Expect upward revisions, especially for energy importers.
- **UK/AU February CPI** (Mar 25) — If Australia prints above 4%, the RBA hiking cycle could extend.
- **Oil prices** — If Hormuz disruption persists, $100+ oil changes the outlook materially.
- **Fed June FOMC** — The dot plot says one cut in 2026. Markets are skeptical.
- **BoE May MPR** — UK inflation at 3.0% is the highest in the G7. The 5-4 vote split in February suggests a cut is close.
