# Inflation Newsletter — 2026-08-23

US inflation eased in July; the Euro Area and Canada moved the other way. The dispersion story is back.

**Korea is the one real move.** Consumer prices rose 2.79% y/y in July, up from 2.2% in March — a 0.59pp gain and the only change this period clearing the materiality threshold. It also puts the print above both available forecasts: the BOK's February projection of 2.1% for 2026 and the IMF's April figure of 2.5%. The BOK held at 2.50% unanimously for a sixth consecutive meeting at that February decision, with GDP growth revised up to 2.0%. That guidance is now six months stale, which makes the next Board meeting the more informative event.

**Euro Area at 2.93%**, up from 2.73% in June. Small, but it moves the run-rate closer to the ECB's own June projection of 3.0% for 2026 — a number the ECB revised up sharply from 1.9% on the Middle East energy shock — and further from the IMF's 2.6%. Rates stay at 2.00%. Canada's 3.03% in July, up 0.23pp, sits above the BoC's July view of 2.5% and its stated expectation of inflation near target.

**Japan and China remain the anchors at the bottom.** Japan printed 1.7% in June; the BoJ's July statement flags a temporary dip below 2% before energy costs push it back up, with the policy rate at 0.75% on an 8–1 vote. China was 0.5% in July, with the 1Y LPR unchanged at 3.00% for ten months.

The April 2026 WEO carries broad upside revisions across advanced economies from energy pass-through, with global inflation at 4.4% for 2026. Australia (4.0%) and South Africa (3.9%) top the country table; the US and UK sit at 3.2%, China at 1.2%.

Two gaps worth tracking. Australia has been stuck at 3.8% since June, with the RBA — which hiked twice this year to 4.10% — forecasting 3.3% for 2026 against the IMF's 4.0%. And South Africa's 5.0% in June runs well above both the SARB's May projection of 4.4% and the IMF's 3.9%, even after the May hike to 7.00%.
