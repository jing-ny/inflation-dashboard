# Inflation Newsletter — 2026-06-01

Headline inflation pushed higher across much of the advanced world this period, as the March Middle East energy shock fed through to prices — and beneath the moves, widening central bank divergence is reshaping the 2026 outlook.

**The Big Picture: Energy Shock Lifts the Advanced-Economy Outlook**

Headline readings climbed in several major economies — the US rose to 3.8% (from 3.3%) and the Euro Area to 3.0% (from 2.6%) — while the dramatic story sits in the forecast revisions. The IMF's April WEO marked up global inflation projections sharply, lifting its 2026 world forecast to 4.4%, with the US revised up 0.8 percentage points and the Euro Area 0.7, while Australia and New Zealand each faced full percentage-point increases — driven by energy pass-through from the March Middle East conflict.

**Policy Divergence Widens**

Central banks are splitting into distinct camps. The Reserve Bank of Australia delivered two consecutive hikes in early 2026, pushing rates to 4.10% as trimmed mean inflation threatens to peak at 3.7% — well above the 2-3% target band through early 2027. Brazil's central bank took an even more aggressive stance, lifting the Selic rate to 14.25% in March as inflation expectations remained stubbornly elevated — its Focus survey sees 2026 inflation at 5.7%, well above the 3% target.

Meanwhile, other major economies are holding steady or cutting. Mexico's Banxico reduced rates to 9.50%, betting that inflation — 4.6% in March — eases back toward target, with staff projecting 3.8% for 2026. Canada's BoC maintains a cautious 2.25% stance despite trade uncertainty from ongoing US tariff reviews.

**The China-Venezuela Extremes**

China's 1.0% March reading reinforces its disinflationary environment, with the People's Bank maintaining an accommodative stance (1-year LPR held at 3.00%). The IMF projects just 1.2% inflation for 2026 — a stark contrast to Venezuela's hyperinflationary 618% that continues to distort regional comparisons despite post-crisis recovery efforts.

**What to Watch**

Three key divergences demand attention: Australia's aggressive tightening cycle versus the broader developed-world pause; Brazil's monetary hawks fighting persistent price pressures; and the growing gap between IMF projections and current central bank forecasts, particularly in energy-sensitive economies where the March energy shock may require further policy recalibration.
