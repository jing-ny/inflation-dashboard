# Inflation Newsletter — 2026-05-10

The inflation landscape stayed largely static in March, but the UK emerged as the sole mover among major economies, with CPI rising to 3.3% from February's 3.0%. This modest uptick runs counter to the Bank of England's February forecast calling for a decline to 2.1% by Q2 2026, creating the period's clearest central bank-reality divergence.

Elsewhere, the major economies held their positions: the US at 3.26%, the Euro Area at 2.6%, and Canada at 2.4%. China's 1.0% March reading remained the lowest among tracked economies, while Japan's February figure of 1.3% continued to reflect the Bank of Japan's cautious approach to monetary normalization.

**Central Bank vs. Reality**

Brazil presents the starkest policy tension, with March inflation at 4.14% while the central bank's most recent guidance projects 4.89% for the full year — a gap that helped justify the Banco Central's aggressive March rate hike to 14.25%. Mexico's 4.59% March reading similarly tracks above Banxico's 3.8% forecast, though the central bank proceeded with a cut to 9.50% in March.

The Euro Area's 2.6% exactly matches the ECB's revised March forecast, a figure that was sharply adjusted upward from 1.9% following Middle East energy disruptions.

**IMF Revisions Signal Broad Energy Impact**

The April 2026 World Economic Outlook delivered significant upward revisions across advanced economies, with the US and Euro Area each seeing +0.8pp and +0.7pp adjustments respectively. Australia and New Zealand faced +1.0pp increases. The IMF now projects global inflation at 4.4% for 2026, with energy pass-through effects from the Middle East conflict driving most revisions.

These adjustments create notable gaps between IMF projections and current readings: Australia's 3.7% current rate sits below the IMF's 4.0% forecast, while India's 3.4% trails the Fund's 4.7% projection.

**What to Watch**

The UK's unexpected March acceleration tests BoE forecasting credibility ahead of upcoming data. Brazil's monetary tightening cycle continues against persistent above-forecast inflation. China's subdued 1.0% reading warrants monitoring for any policy response signals.
