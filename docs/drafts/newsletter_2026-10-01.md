# Inflation Newsletter — 2026-10-01

Only one country in the panel printed a lower number this round. Australia's July CPI eased to 3.5% from 3.8% in June, a 0.3pp step down. Everywhere else, the August prints were flat or higher.

The Euro Area did the heaviest lifting: 3.23% in August against 2.93% in July, a 0.3pp rise that puts headline inflation more than a point above the ECB's 2.00% policy rate. The ECB's September projections already carry 3.0% for 2026, revised up from 1.9% to 2.6% on the Middle East energy price surge, with rates held. The gap between a 2% policy rate and a 3%+ inflation path is the single most uncomfortable number on this page.

South Korea's move is the one to log. August CPI at 3.09%, up 0.3pp from 2.79% in July, now sits a full point above the BOK's February projection of 2.1% for 2026. That forecast is seven months old and looks increasingly stale; the next BOK revision matters more than the next print.

China ticked up to 0.8% from 0.5% — a material change in a series that has spent the year near zero, though still far below the IMF's 1.2% for 2026 and far below everyone else. Japan held at 2.0%, the cleanest target-consistent reading in the set, with the BoJ at 0.75% in July and signalling further hikes if its outlook holds.

The US was effectively unchanged at 3.40%, Canada unchanged at 3.03%, the UK up 0.2pp to 3.1%, and South Africa up 0.1pp to 4.4% — the highest headline here, and squarely on SARB's May projection of 4.4% for 2026 after its hike to 7.00%.

The April WEO vintage sits awkwardly against several central banks. The IMF has the US at 3.2% for 2026 against the Fed's September 3.7%; the Euro Area at 2.6% against the ECB's 3.0%. In both cases the newer central bank vintage is higher, which tells you which direction the October WEO is likely to travel.

Watch two things: whether the Euro Area's August rise extends into September, and how far the BOK moves its 2.1% when it next updates.
