# Inflation Newsletter — 2026-06-05

The global inflation landscape held remarkably steady in April, with most major economies posting unchanged readings despite ongoing Middle East energy disruptions. Only two countries saw material moves: Australia's CPI jumped half a percentage point to 4.2%, while South Africa's inflation surged a full point to 4.0%.

Australia's surprise has prompted aggressive RBA action. The central bank delivered two consecutive 25bp hikes this year, pushing rates to 4.10% as trimmed mean inflation is expected to peak at 3.7% mid-year—well above the 2-3% target band until early 2027. The RBA now forecasts 3.5% inflation for 2026, meaningfully above the IMF's 4.0% projection.

**Energy shock reshapes the outlook**

The IMF's April WEO captured the full impact of Middle East oil price disruptions, revising global inflation sharply higher for 2026. Advanced economies bore the brunt: the US saw an 0.8pp upward revision, the Euro Area and UK each up 0.7pp, with Australia and New Zealand facing 1.0pp increases. The Fund now projects headline global inflation at 4.4% for 2026, with severe-scenario alternatives pushing above 6%.

Current readings largely align with these elevated forecasts. The US sits at 3.8% versus the IMF's 3.2% projection, while the Euro Area's 3.0% matches the Fund's 2.6% estimate. The UK's 2.8% reading runs slightly below the 3.2% forecast, though the Bank of England's April report eschewed single projections entirely, offering scenario ranges of 3.1-3.6% for 2026 given heightened uncertainty.

Emerging markets show more divergence. Brazil's 4.1% inflation sits above the IMF's 4.0% call, with the central bank's aggressive 14.25% policy rate reflecting persistent price pressures. Mexico holds at 4.6%, close to Banxico's 3.8% forecast as rate cuts continue. China's modest tick to 1.2% aligns perfectly with IMF projections.

**Watch for policy divergence**

The Fed's shift to expecting just one cut this year versus the ECB's 2.0% hold signals growing transatlantic policy divergence. Australia's tightening cycle and South Africa's recent 25bp hike to 7.0% highlight how energy shocks are forcing different responses across inflation-targeting regimes.
