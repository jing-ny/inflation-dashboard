# Inflation Newsletter — 2026-04-22

## Key Changes

Material inflation moves this period came from both sides of the Atlantic and Latin America. The **Euro Area** saw the most significant acceleration, with CPI jumping 0.7pp to **2.6%** ↑ in March—exactly matching the ECB's revised 2026 forecast after energy price disruptions in the Middle East. **Canada** followed with a notable 0.6pp increase to **2.4%** ↑, though this remains aligned with the Bank of Canada's 2.0% target expectation.

**Mexico** inflation surged 0.82pp to **4.59%** ↑, moving closer to the upper bound of Banxico's 2-4% tolerance band, while the central bank's 3.8% forecast suggests continued upward pressure. In contrast, **Brazil** provided relief with inflation declining 0.92pp to **4.14%** ↓, though still well above the BCB's 3% target amid aggressive monetary tightening (Selic at 14.25%).

**China** continues its disinflationary trend, dropping 0.3pp to **1.0%** ↓—significantly below both the IMF's 1.2% projection and the PBoC's apparent comfort zone.

## Trend Summary

Advanced economies show mixed signals. The **United States** (3.26% →) and **United Kingdom** (3.0% →) held steady, while **Australia** saw a modest decline to 3.7% ↓. **Japan** remains anchored at 1.3% →, below the BoJ's 2.0% target despite recent rate hikes to 0.75%.

Asian economies generally exhibit stability: **South Korea** (2.2% ↑), **India** (3.4% ↑), and **Singapore** (1.2% →) showing minimal movement. **New Zealand** stayed flat at 3.1% → despite RBNZ expectations of temporary elevation above target.

The standout remains **Venezuela** at 617.9% →, reflecting ongoing post-hyperinflation dynamics with limited data reliability.

## What to Watch

Central bank forecasts increasingly diverge from IMF projections, particularly for the Euro Area (ECB: 2.6% vs IMF: 1.9%) and Australia (RBA: 3.7% vs IMF: 3.0%), suggesting heightened uncertainty around energy and domestic price pressures.

Mexico's accelerating inflation against Banxico's cutting cycle warrants attention, while Brazil's disinflation progress faces headwinds from elevated policy rates. China's persistent undershooting raises questions about domestic demand recovery.

Energy-driven revisions in Europe and potential spillovers from Middle East disruptions remain key near-term risks across developed markets.
