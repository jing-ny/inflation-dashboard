# Inflation Newsletter — 2026-04-22

## Key Changes

Material inflation moves this period came from both sides of the Atlantic and Latin America. The **Euro Area** saw the most significant acceleration, with CPI jumping 0.7pp to **2.6%** ↑ in March—exactly matching the ECB's revised 2026 forecast after energy price disruptions in the Middle East. **Canada** followed with a notable 0.6pp increase to **2.4%** ↑, though this remains aligned with the Bank of Canada's 2.0% target expectation.

**Mexico** inflation surged 0.82pp to **4.59%** ↑, moving closer to the upper bound of Banxico's 2-4% tolerance band, while the central bank's 3.8% forecast suggests continued upward pressure. In contrast, **Brazil** provided relief with inflation declining 0.92pp to **4.14%** ↓, though still well above the BCB's 3% target amid aggressive monetary tightening (Selic at 14.25%).

**China** continues its disinflationary trend, dropping 0.3pp to **1.0%** ↓—significantly below both the IMF's 1.2% projection and the PBoC's apparent comfort zone.

## Trend Summary

Advanced economies show mixed signals. The **United States** accelerated to **3.26%** ↑ in March (from 2.41% in Feb), the largest monthly jump of the current cycle, driven by an 11% energy index surge. **Australia** (Feb data) ticked down to 3.7% ↓ and the **United Kingdom** held at 3.0% in its Feb reading (UK March release was today — not yet in data). **Japan** remains at 1.3% → (Feb data), below the BoJ's 2.0% target; BoJ policy rate 0.75%.

Asian economies generally exhibit stability: **South Korea** (2.2% ↑ from 2.0%), **India** (3.4% ↑ from 3.21%), and **Singapore** (1.2% → Feb data). **New Zealand** Q1 2026 held at 3.1% →; note RBNZ guidance points to ~4.2% for Q2 on Mid-East energy pass-through.

The standout remains **Venezuela** at 617.9% → (Feb, irregular reporting).

## IMF WEO April 2026: broad upside revisions

The IMF released its April 2026 World Economic Outlook on April 14 — the first full WEO reflecting the Middle East conflict oil-price shock. Compared to the January 2026 Update, 2026 inflation forecasts were revised **upward for 12 of the 15 countries we track**, with the largest revisions in advanced economies: **Australia and New Zealand +1.0pp each** (to 4.0% and 3.1%), **US +0.8pp** (to 3.2%), **Singapore +0.8pp** (to 2.3%), **UK and Euro Area +0.7pp each** (to 3.2% and 2.6%). Only Japan (−0.1pp to 2.2%) and Brazil (−0.5pp to 4.0%) were revised lower. Global headline inflation was raised to 4.4% for 2026, with adverse-scenario 5.4% and severe-scenario >6%.

## What to Watch

With the IMF revision, **most central banks now forecast 2026 inflation below IMF** — a reversal from the January baseline when CBs generally sat above IMF. The widest gaps (CB minus IMF, 2026):

- UK: BoE 2.3% vs IMF 3.2% — **−0.9pp**
- Singapore: MAS 1.5% vs IMF 2.3% — **−0.8pp**
- India: RBI 4.0% vs IMF 4.7% — **−0.7pp**
- New Zealand: RBNZ 2.5% vs IMF 3.1% — **−0.6pp**
- South Africa: SARB 3.3% vs IMF 3.9% — **−0.6pp**

Euro Area is the notable alignment (ECB 2.6% = IMF 2.6%) after the ECB revised its March projection up to match the new energy picture. **Brazil is the only large economy where the CB sits above IMF** (BCB 5.7% vs IMF 4.0%, +1.7pp), reflecting BCB's hawkish stance with Selic at 14.25%.

Upcoming: UK March CPI (released today; not yet verified), JP March (Apr 24), SG March (~Apr 23), AU March (~Apr 28), ZA March (pending). Mexico's accelerating inflation against Banxico's cutting cycle warrants continued attention.
