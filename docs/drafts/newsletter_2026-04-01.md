# Inflation Newsletter — 2026-04-01

## Key Changes

All major economies showed stable inflation readings in the latest data snapshot, with no material changes (≥0.3pp) reported across any region. Current rates remain: US 2.41%, Euro Area 1.9%, UK 3.0%, Canada 1.8%, Australia 3.8%, and New Zealand 3.1%. Among emerging markets, Brazil leads at 5.06%, while Japan and China both register 1.3%. Venezuela continues to experience extreme hyperinflation at 617.9%.

## Trend Summary

The widespread stability masks significant divergences in central bank outlooks and policy stances. Australia's RBA has turned notably hawkish, hiking rates twice in 2026 to 4.10% after inflation surprised to the upside, with trimmed mean inflation expected to peak at 3.7% mid-2026—well above the 2-3% target band until early 2027.

Brazil maintains the most restrictive policy at 14.25%, with the central bank projecting 5.7% inflation for 2026, significantly above the 3% target. This contrasts sharply with low-inflation environments in Asia, where Singapore expects just 1.5% inflation and Japan projects 2.0% despite recent rate hikes to 0.75%.

Energy disruptions from Middle East tensions have prompted upward revisions from major central banks. The ECB sharply revised its 2026 forecast from 1.9% to 2.6%, while the Fed raised PCE projections to 2.7%. However, the UK's BoE bucked this trend, lowering expectations to 2.3% due to energy price relief from Budget 2025 measures.

Several central banks maintain restrictive stances despite stable current readings. Mexico's Banxico holds rates at 9.50% while projecting 3.8% inflation, and South Korea's BOK keeps policy at 2.50% for the sixth consecutive meeting.

## What to Watch

Monitor whether the current stability proves durable given central bank concerns about energy price pressures. The divergence between ECB/Fed upward revisions and BoE's more optimistic outlook reflects different regional energy policy impacts. Australia's aggressive tightening cycle and Brazil's persistent above-target inflation warrant close attention as potential early indicators of broader price pressures.

Trade uncertainty, particularly around US tariff policies affecting Canada and other trading partners, adds another layer of complexity to inflation dynamics. The contrast between current stability and central bank forecast revisions suggests underlying tensions that could emerge in coming months.
