# Inflation Newsletter — 2026-04-21

## Key Changes

**Euro Area** inflation surged 0.7pp to 2.6% in March, marking the most significant move among major economies. The European Central Bank had recently revised its 2026 forecast upward to 2.6% due to Middle East energy price disruptions, and current data now aligns with that projection—well above the IMF's more conservative 1.9% estimate.

**Canada** saw inflation jump 0.6pp to 2.4% in March, bringing it slightly above the Bank of Canada's 2.0% target and matching both central bank and IMF forecasts for 2026. The BoC has noted elevated trade uncertainty due to ongoing US tariff reviews.

**China** moved in the opposite direction, with inflation declining 0.3pp to 1.0% in March. This puts current rates below both the IMF's 1.2% projection and well under the People's Bank of China's apparent comfort zone, as the 1-year LPR has remained unchanged at 3.00% for ten consecutive months.

## Trend Summary

Most major economies showed stability in their latest readings. The **United States** (3.26%), **United Kingdom** (3.0%), and **Australia** (3.7%) held steady, though all remain above their respective central bank targets. Australia's Reserve Bank has been particularly hawkish, hiking rates twice in 2026 to 4.10% after inflation surprises.

**Brazil** continues to face the highest inflation among major economies at 5.06%, well above its 3% target, prompting the central bank to raise rates to 14.25%. **Japan** remains at the low end at 1.3%, with the Bank of Japan maintaining its gradual tightening approach at 0.75%.

**Venezuela** remains an extreme outlier at 617.9%, reflecting its ongoing post-hyperinflation recovery, though this represents a dramatic improvement from the ~475% seen in 2025.

## What to Watch

The divergence between Euro Area and Chinese inflation trends highlights different regional pressures—energy-driven increases in Europe versus deflationary concerns in China. Central bank forecasts generally exceed IMF projections for 2026, particularly in Australia (3.7% vs 3.0%) and the Euro Area (2.6% vs 1.9%), suggesting policymakers see more persistent inflationary pressures.

Energy price volatility from Middle East developments continues to influence forecasts, while trade policy uncertainty adds another layer of complexity for inflation expectations globally.
