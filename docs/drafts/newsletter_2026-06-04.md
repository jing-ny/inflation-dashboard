# Inflation Newsletter — 2026-06-04

The inflation landscape held remarkably steady this month, with most major economies showing zero movement in their latest readings. But beneath the surface calm, two countries delivered sharp upward surprises that signal broader pressures may be building.

Australia jumped half a percentage point to 4.2% in April, up from 3.7% in February. South Africa saw an even steeper climb, rising a full percentage point to 4.0%. Both moves prompted immediate central bank responses—the RBA delivered two rate hikes this year, pushing rates to 4.10%, while South Africa's central bank lifted rates to 7.00% in May as Middle East energy shocks clouded the inflation outlook.

Elsewhere, the picture was one of entrenched stability. The Euro Area held at 3.0%, Mexico at 4.59%, and Brazil at 4.14%—all unchanged from prior readings. China's 1.0% in March and Japan's 1.3% in February represent the global low end, though both remain well below their central bank targets.

The IMF's April World Economic Outlook captured the energy shock's broader impact, revising 2026 inflation forecasts sharply higher across advanced economies. The US projection jumped 0.8 percentage points, the Euro Area and UK each saw 0.7-point increases, while Australia and New Zealand faced full 1.0-point upward revisions. The Fund now sees global inflation at 4.4% for 2026, with a severe-scenario alternative pushing above 6%.

Central bank forecasts paint a more mixed picture than the IMF's broad-brush revisions. Australia's RBA expects inflation to peak at 3.7% mid-year before falling back into the 2-3% target band by early 2027. South Africa's central bank projects a longer journey, with inflation averaging 4.4% in 2026 and 3.7% in 2027 before reaching the 3% target in 2028.

The divergence between current readings and central bank projections is most pronounced in Brazil, where the BCB's 5.09% forecast sits well above the current 4.14% print, and Mexico, where Banxico's 3.8% projection suggests further convergence toward target.

Watch for Australia's next CPI release and whether South Africa's energy-driven pressures spread to other commodity-sensitive economies.
